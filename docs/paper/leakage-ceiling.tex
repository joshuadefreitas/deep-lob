\author{Joshua de Freitas}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We train a short-horizon limit-order-book classifier on synthetic data in which
future price movement is independent of every feature by construction. Under the
standard evaluation recipe --- sliding windows at stride one, a random
train/validation split, and normalisation fitted on the full dataset --- the
model scores $+26.8$ points above its majority-class baseline at a
$20$-step horizon. Under a purged and embargoed split, on identical data with the
same architecture and the same seeds, it scores $-8.4$ points ---
\emph{below} baseline, which is the correct result when nothing is learnable.
The $35$-point difference is produced entirely by how the validation set was
drawn. Both figures are means over five independent generator paths with twenty
split seeds each ($900$ runs); across paths the leaky excess ranges
$+26.0$ to $+28.2$ and the purged excess $-14.6$ to $-3.3$, so the sign of each is a property of the protocol rather than
of the realisation.

We then derive a closed-form expression for the accuracy a given split geometry
makes available. It depends only on the window stride, the label horizon, the
labelling threshold and the train fraction; no property of the model enters it.
It is an approximation rather than a proved bound --- an oracle memoriser sits
just below it at $h \in \{5,10\}$ and just above it at $h=20$ --- and it tracks
that oracle to within $0.56$ points at every horizon, averaged over six
independent generator paths. The derivation is further checked
against $105$ measurements spanning $20$ independent generator paths,
five window sizes, five thresholds and five train fractions.

The derivation is prescriptive. With stride $s$ and horizon $h$ the governing
correlation is $\rho(s)=\max\!\left(0,(h-s)/h\right)$, which is exactly zero at
$s \geq h$. Overlap leakage therefore does not merely diminish as stride
increases --- it disappears once stride reaches the horizon. We confirm this,
and quantify its cost: the usable sample shrinks by a factor of $s$.

Separately, we find that the pipeline under study is not reproducible. Neither the
split draw, the weight initialisation nor the batch order is seeded, so repeated
runs of identical code on identical data differ materially. A model comparison
decided by a margin smaller than that spread is indistinguishable from running
the same model twice.

All figures regenerate from committed scripts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Data leakage --- the presence of information in training that would not be
available at prediction time --- is a well documented cause of overstated
machine learning results. Kapoor and Narayanan~\cite{kapoor2023} surveyed
seventeen fields, identified $329$ affected papers, and produced an eight-type
taxonomy, and tooling exists to detect some leakage classes
automatically~\cite{talagala2024}. In financial time series specifically, purging and embargoing are
established remedies~\cite{lopezdeprado2018}, and the broader problem of
backtest overfitting has been treated at length~\cite{bailey2014}.

None of that is in dispute here, and none of it is what this paper contributes.

The difficulty with demonstrating leakage on real data is that manufactured
skill and genuine skill are indistinguishable in the output. A practitioner
shown that purged cross-validation lowers their score has a ready and not
unreasonable reply: purging is conservative, it discards legitimate
short-horizon signal along with the leak, and the lower number reflects the
remedy rather than an honest measurement. That objection cannot be settled on
data where signal might exist.

It can be settled on data where signal cannot. We therefore construct a
\emph{negative control}: a synthetic order book whose mid price is an
i.i.d.\ multiplicative random walk and whose order sizes are independent draws,
with no mechanism connecting either to future price. Predictive skill is not
merely absent --- it is impossible. Every point of measured accuracy above the
majority-class baseline is, by construction, an artifact of the evaluation
procedure, and no appeal to destroyed signal is available.

This yields two results. The first is a measurement: how much predictability the
standard recipe invents from nothing. The second, and the one we consider the
contribution, is that this quantity has a closed form. Given the geometry of the
windows and the correlation structure of the labels, the accuracy available to
be manufactured can be computed before any model is trained --- and the same
expression states the condition under which it becomes zero.

\section{The negative control}

\subsection{Generator}

Mid prices follow
\begin{equation}
p_t = p_{t-1}\left(1 + \varepsilon_t\right), \qquad
\varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2) \ \text{i.i.d.}, \qquad
\sigma = 3\times10^{-4}.
\end{equation}
Three bid and three ask levels are placed around the mid at a spread of one to
three ticks, and every level's size is an independent uniform integer draw. No
size, spread or level price depends on any future price. Forward returns are
independent of all features.

That this holds is enforced rather than asserted: a permutation test over
non-overlapping spans, Bonferroni corrected across features, runs in continuous
integration and fails if any feature acquires a detectable relationship with the
forward return. The test has been observed to fail when a signal is deliberately
introduced.

\subsection{Windows and labels}

Features are built into sliding windows of $W = 100$ rows at stride one:
window $t$ spans rows $t,\dots,t+W-1$. The label is the sign of the forward
return over horizon $h$, thresholded at $\theta = 5\times10^{-4}$:
\begin{equation}
y_t = \begin{cases}
+1 & r_t > \theta\\
0 & |r_t| \leq \theta\\
-1 & r_t < -\theta
\end{cases}
\qquad
r_t = \frac{p_{t+W-1+h} - p_{t+W-1}}{p_{t+W-1}} .
\end{equation}

Two consequences of stride-one construction matter throughout. Adjacent windows
share $W-1$ of $W$ input rows. And adjacent labels are signs of forward returns
computed over spans that share $h-1$ of $h$ increments.

Three splitting protocols are compared, holding architecture, data, optimiser
and epoch count fixed.

Purging removes training windows whose raw-row span reaches into the validation
region; the embargo additionally drops a buffer of validation windows after the
cut~\cite{lopezdeprado2018}.

Every run seeds the split draw, the weight initialisation and the batch order,
so the study is bit-reproducible even though the pipeline it studies is not
(Section~\ref{sec:repro}).

\paragraph{Configuration span.} Five independent generator paths $\times$ three
protocols $\times$ three horizons $\times$ twenty split seeds: $900$ runs. Every
number reported below is a mean over generator paths, quoted with its across-path
standard deviation, and every claim is additionally checked on each path
separately (Section~\ref{sec:perpath}). The study is \emph{not} varied across
window size, labelling threshold, train fraction, optimiser, epoch count, row
count or architecture; Section~\ref{sec:robust} varies the first three for the
derivation alone, and Section~\ref{sec:limits} states what that leaves open.

Accuracy is reported as \textbf{excess over the majority-class baseline computed
on the same validation set}. The baseline moves from $0.575$ to $0.398$ across
horizons because the class balance shifts with $h$, so raw accuracies are not
comparable between rows.

\section{The ceiling}
\label{sec:ceiling}

The preceding section measures what one architecture achieves. A different
question admits a closed-form answer: given the geometry alone, how much
accuracy is \emph{available} to be manufactured?

Adjacent windows have forward returns that are sums of $h$ i.i.d.\ increments
sharing $h-1$ of them, so
\begin{equation}
\rho = \operatorname{corr}(r_t, r_{t+1}) = \frac{h-1}{h},
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
which increases with the horizon. Writing $z = \theta/(\sigma\sqrt{h})$ for the
threshold in units of the $h$-step return's standard deviation, and $\Phi_2$ for
the standard bivariate normal CDF at correlation $\rho$, the probability that
two adjacent windows carry the same label is
\begin{equation}
A(h) = \underbrace{\Phi_2(-z,-z)}_{\text{both down}}
     + \underbrace{\Phi_2(z,z) - 2\Phi_2(-z,z) + \Phi_2(-z,-z)}_{\text{both flat}}
     + \underbrace{1 - 2\Phi(z) + \Phi_2(z,z)}_{\text{both up}} .
\end{equation}

A memoriser copies the label of its \emph{nearest} training window, which is not
always the adjacent one. Under a random split at train fraction $f$, the distance
$d$ to the nearest training neighbour satisfies
\begin{equation}
\Pr(d > k) = (1-f)^{2k}, \qquad
\Pr(d = k) = (1-f)^{2(k-1)} - (1-f)^{2k},
\end{equation}
and a neighbour $d$ windows away shares $h-d$ of $h$ increments, so
$\rho_d = \max(0, (h-d)/h)$. The ceiling is the expectation of the agreement
probability over that distance distribution:
\begin{equation}
C(h) = \sum_{d \geq 1} \Pr(d)\, A(\rho_d).
\label{eq:ceiling}
\end{equation}
Nothing about the model appears in~\eqref{eq:ceiling}. It is computable before
any training run.

An earlier version of this derivation collapsed every case beyond $d=1$ to the
majority class. That is wrong, and diagnosably so: it under-predicted by $0.094$
at $h=20$, $f=0.5$ --- where a quarter of validation windows have no adjacent
twin --- with the error decaying monotonically to $0.004$ at $f=0.9$. The
train-fraction sweep in Section~\ref{sec:robust} was what exposed it.

\subsection{Verification}

We evaluate an \emph{oracle}: for each validation window, predict the label of
the temporally nearest training window. The neighbour is supplied rather than
learned, which isolates the correlation model from the separate question of
whether a learner could locate its twin.

The expression binds only under a random split. Under \texttt{chronological} and
\texttt{purged\_embargoed} the nearest training window is the \emph{same} window
for every validation example, so the strategy degenerates to a constant
predictor and scores at or below baseline: across $36$ measurements --- six
generator paths $\times$ three horizons $\times$ two honest protocols --- the
oracle's excess over baseline runs from $-0.38$ to exactly $0.00$ and is
positive in none of them.
Copying one's neighbour is useful only when the neighbour has been scattered
into the training set.

\subsection{Availability is not exploitation}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.72\textwidth]{../figures/fig3-available-vs-taken.pdf}
\caption{The shaded region is the leak the split makes available. The trained
network takes none of it at $h=5$ and $62\%$ at $h=20$.}
\label{fig:taken}

Measured accuracy can be read as the fraction of available leak a given model
recovers. Averaged over generator paths: none at $h=5$, $45\%$ at $h=10$,
$62\%$ at $h=20$. At $h=5$ there are $14.5$ points of leak available and this
architecture takes none of them --- it is $0.6$ points \emph{below} its baseline,
which is within the across-path spread of $0.7$ points.

The ceiling therefore describes the \emph{exposure} a split geometry creates.
Whether a particular model converts that exposure into a headline number is a
separate, model-dependent question. Both are worth knowing, and only the first
can be computed in advance.

For completeness: a 1-nearest-neighbour classifier in raw feature space scores
$0.29$--$0.45$ across $360$ runs on six generator paths, below baseline
everywhere. Euclidean distance over price-level features
locates windows at similar \emph{price levels}, not temporal twins. The trained
network's performance is therefore not naive distance matching.

\section{The remedy}

Equation~\eqref{eq:rho} assumed stride one. With stride $s$, retained windows
are $s$ rows apart and their forward returns share $h-s$ of $h$ increments:
\begin{equation}
\rho(s) = \max\!\left(0, \frac{h-s}{h}\right).
\label{eq:rhos}
\end{equation}
At $s \geq h$ this is exactly zero: retained windows share no increments, their
labels are independent, and copying a neighbour cannot beat the baseline.
Overlap leakage does not diminish asymptotically --- it terminates.

Two consequences deserve separation.

\paragraph{The rule.} Set stride $\geq$ horizon and overlap leakage is gone
rather than reduced. At intermediate strides~\eqref{eq:rhos}
and~\eqref{eq:ceiling} quantify the remaining exposure, so the choice can be
made numerically.

\paragraph{The cost.} Stride $s$ divides the usable sample by $s$: $4881$
windows become $245$ at $h=20$. This is the actual trade --- leakage against
sample size --- and it is why stride-one windowing is common. The formula does
not remove the trade; it prices it.

Beyond $s=h$ the memoriser scores \emph{below} baseline, because with
independent labels copying a neighbour yields $\sum_i p_i^2 < \max_i p_i$. This
is predicted behaviour and serves as an independent check on the derivation.

\paragraph{The verification is weakest where the remedy is strongest.} Raising
the stride divides the sample by $s$, which reduces the validation set the
remedy is verified on by the same factor. At $5{,}000$ rows and $h=s=20$ only
$49$ validation windows remain, so a single window is worth $2.04$ accuracy
points; on one of five generator paths the measured excess came out at
$+0.043$ --- two windows --- against a claim that it should be non-positive. At
$20{,}000$ rows the same cell has $199$ validation windows and all five paths
are negative. Pooling all $255$ measured cells, the closed form's error against
the oracle falls monotonically with validation size: $0.024$ mean below $60$
windows, $0.0065$ above $400$. Restricted to cells with at least $150$
validation windows the mean error is $0.0095$ and the maximum $0.051$.

The apparent breakdown is therefore a property of the measurement, not of the
remedy. It is reported because a single-path run at $5{,}000$ rows would have
shown a clean result and concealed it.

\section{Robustness of the derivation}
\label{sec:robust}

The ceiling contains stride, horizon, threshold, per-step volatility and train
fraction. It does \emph{not} contain the window size $W$, which is a falsifiable
prediction: widening the feature window changes what the model sees but not how
correlated the labels of neighbouring windows are, and the leak lives in the
labels.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering

\midrule

Window size & $W \in \{25,\dots,400\}$ & \textbf{0.0053} & 0.0031 \\
Threshold & $\theta \in [10^{-4}, 2\times10^{-3}]$ & 0.0223 & 0.0081 \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Closed form against measured oracle, $105$ configurations.}
\end{table}

A sixteen-fold change in window size moves the oracle by less than $0.006$ while
the prediction is held fixed. The prediction survives. Twenty independent
generator paths give a mean absolute error of $0.007$, so the result is not a
property of one realisation.

\section{Reproducibility of the pipeline under study}
\label{sec:repro}

The pipeline seeds neither the split draw, the weight initialisation, nor the
batch order. Repeated runs of identical code on identical data therefore differ.
Six unseeded repetitions of one configuration (\texttt{random\_split}, $h=20$,
generator seed $42$) yield validation accuracies spanning $0.5036$ to $0.7134$
(mean $0.6395$, sd $0.0830$) and all-window accuracies spanning $0.5206$ to
$0.7408$ (mean $0.6712$, sd $0.0864$); runs and metrics are committed in
\texttt{results/study-multiseed/summary.json} under \texttt{unseeded\_arm}. An
earlier estimate of $0.67$--$0.75$ predated the committed harness and is
superseded by this artifact.

Stated separately from the leakage result because it is independent and applies
more broadly: \textbf{a model comparison decided by a margin smaller than the
pipeline's own run-to-run spread is indistinguishable from running the same model
twice.} This
includes the architecture comparison recorded in the source repository. Seed
sensitivity of this magnitude is consistent with reports
elsewhere~\cite{picard2021}.

Three lines of seeding render the pipeline bit-deterministic.

\section{The normalisation leak, measured}

The pipeline also fits its feature normalisation on the whole dataset: each size
column is divided by its maximum over all rows, computed before any split
exists. An earlier version of this paper described that leak as "presumed small,
not measured." It is neither.

then applied to both arms --- and pairing on generator path, split seed and
split, so that only the scaling differs:

The direction is the opposite of the one assumed. \textbf{Removing the
normalisation leak makes the leaky protocol perform better}, by $4.3$ points at
$h{=}10$ and $7.3$ at $h{=}20$. Dividing by a global maximum compresses the size
features into a narrow band and flattens their structure; standardising each
feature to unit variance preserves it, and a model that can see more structure
identifies its near-duplicate twin more easily.

So the two defects were never independent contributions to be summed. One was
partially masking the other, and the honest reading is:

\begin{quote}
Correct feature scaling amplifies a leak. It does not create one.
\end{quote}

Where a leak exists to exploit, better scaling exploits more of it. Where none
exists --- the honest protocols --- the effect is null or slightly negative, and
in no case does it lift a cell above baseline. \textbf{All $36$ purged
combinations, six generator paths $\times$ three horizons $\times$ two scalings,
remain below their validation-majority baseline}; the narrowest margin is
$-2.69$ points.

One consequence for the headline: with correct train-only scaling,
\texttt{random\_split} at $h{=}20$ reports $72.1\%$ against a $39.8\%$ baseline.
Fixing one methodological error makes the other one worse.

\paragraph{And $h{=}5$ is not a negative control.} An earlier draft treated the
$h{=}5$ cell --- where the leaky protocol sits at its baseline --- as evidence
that overlap alone is insufficient. It is not evidence of anything. Across ten
path-and-scaling combinations, nine excesses are statistically indistinguishable
from zero, the single exception is what ten comparisons produce by chance, and
the sign varies by generator path. The correct statement is that at $h{=}5$ the
effect is too small to resolve at this sample size, and we can neither confirm
nor exclude it.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No published result is shown to be incorrect.} We show what a
procedure can manufacture on data without signal. Whether any particular result
suffers the same effect requires that result's data and code.
\item \textbf{This is not a measurement of real markets.} The generator is a
random walk without microstructure --- appropriate for a negative control, a
limitation for external validity.
\item \textbf{The normalisation leak is now measured} rather than presumed, and
it does not behave additively --- see Section~6. The three defects are not
separable channels and the paper does not decompose them as such.
\item \textbf{The ceiling assumes i.i.d.\ increments.} Real returns exhibit
volatility clustering and heavy tails; $\rho = (h-s)/h$ is exact only under the
generator used here.
\item \textbf{Predicted class balance} matches measurement within $0.3$ standard
deviations at $h \in \{5,10\}$ across $20$ independent paths, and
under-predicts the majority share by approximately $1.3$ standard deviations at
$h=20$. This affects the $M(h)$ term in~\eqref{eq:ceiling}, not the verified
$A(h)$ term.
\item \textbf{One generator family and one architecture.} A second model family
is untested, so the fraction of the available leak a model recovers may be
architecture-specific.
\item \textbf{Configuration span.} The protocol comparison spans five generator
paths $\times$ twenty split seeds; the ceiling six paths; the stride sweep five
paths $\times$ two generator lengths; the normalisation comparison five paths
$\times$ ten split seeds, plus a twenty-seed reference path. Not varied: window
size in the trained arms, labelling threshold, train fraction, optimiser, epoch
count, architecture.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

On data constructed to contain no predictable structure, the standard evaluation
recipe for time-series machine learning reports $64\%$ accuracy against a
$38\%$ baseline. The honest protocol reports $33\%$ against
$41\%$. Averaged over five generator paths and twenty split seeds each,
and with the same sign on every one of those paths, the difference is attributable
entirely to the evaluation design; because the data contains no signal, the usual
defence of the leaky protocol is unavailable.

The quantity has a closed form. The accuracy a split geometry makes available
depends on stride, horizon, threshold and train fraction, and on nothing about
the model. That expression is verified against measurement across $105$
configurations spanning twenty generator paths, five window sizes, five
thresholds and five train fractions, and it states its own remedy: at stride
greater than or equal to the label horizon, the leak is zero.
